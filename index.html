Research direction

Gesture Interaction × Narrative Lighting × Digital Heritage

Grounded in HCI and embodied interaction, my research combines markerless gesture interaction with narrative lighting, transforming digital heritage from passive viewing into embodied meaning-making.

01 / GESTURE
Embodied Interaction

Gesture is treated as part of cultural understanding rather than merely as input commands: body movement, visual attention, and spatial position jointly constitute the experience.

02 / LIGHTING
Narrative Lighting

Programmable physical light serves as a narrative medium — guiding attention, providing process feedback, shaping atmosphere, and building symbolic associations in response to visitors’ movements.

03 / HERITAGE
Digital Cultural Heritage

I have developed gesture-interaction and VR projects around bronze artifacts, Dunhuang murals, and traditional patterns. As director of the Digital Intangible Cultural Heritage Design and Production teaching and research office, I explore new interaction modes for communicating cultural heritage — turning viewers into participants.

Research background

Digital technology has expanded how museums document, restore, and communicate cultural heritage, yet many digital heritage experiences still relegate visitors to the role of spectators. Embodied interaction offers an alternative: engaging with heritage through bodily action. This research focuses on vision-based, markerless, mid-air gesture interaction and treats physical light in the exhibition space as a programmable narrative medium — one that guides attention, provides process feedback, shapes atmosphere, and carries symbolic meaning. How gesture and physical lighting jointly participate in meaning-making remains underexplored.

Research questions
01
How does gesture-driven narrative lighting affect museum visitors’ experience?
This question focuses on spatial presence, cultural learning, emotional engagement, and exploratory behavior, measuring the added value of narrative lighting over static lighting.
02
How can culturally grounded gesture–lighting mappings be developed?
This question translates core lighting functions — guiding attention, providing process feedback, shaping atmosphere, and building symbolic associations — into gesture–lighting relationships that visitors can understand and learn.
03
Which design principles can support the deployment of such installations in real museum settings?
This question combines participatory design, controlled experiments, and field deployment to develop a framework that balances accessibility, cultural responsibility, and deployability.
